Emotion diary: how to start in one minute a day

An emotion diary means noting down, every day, how you felt and why. You don’t need to write much: one emotion and one line is already a diary.

What is an emotion diary?

It’s a simple record of how you feel. Instead of telling everything that happened, like a classic diary, you focus on what you felt: calm, joy, tiredness, nostalgia… and what caused it.

Over time, that record becomes a map. You start to see which days are good for you, what weighs on you and how your mood changes from one week to the next.

Why keep one?

  • To name what you feel. Sometimes just naming it makes it a little lighter.
  • To spot patterns. What does you good and what doesn’t tends to repeat, and once it’s written down you can see it.
  • To remember. A year from now you can come back to this week and read how you were.
  • To pause for a minute. Writing it down is a small moment just for you.

How to start in 1 minute a day

  1. Pick a fixed moment. At night before bed, or in the morning with your coffee. What matters is that it’s always the same.
  2. Name the emotion. One or two at most: “calm”, “restless”, “joy”. If you’re not sure, pick the closest one.
  3. Write one line about why. “Long walk with my sister.” “Rushing all day at work.” That’s enough.
  4. Once a week, look back. Which colours came up most? What did the good days have in common?

Examples

Tuesday · CalmLong walk after lunch. It had been a while since I walked without rushing.
Wednesday · RestlessA meeting that ran over and the feeling of not getting to everything.
Saturday · JoyDinner with friends at home. We laughed so much.

Frequently asked questions

How much time does it take?

One minute a day is enough. Consistency matters more than writing a lot.

Paper or phone?

Both work. On your phone it’s always with you, and you can see your patterns in charts without doing any maths.

What if I don’t know what I’m feeling?

Pick the closest emotion, or describe how your body feels: tired, tense, light. It gets easier with practice.
